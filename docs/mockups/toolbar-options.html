Toolbar options

Where the page navigator and zoom controls could go, with room for the search box and markup tools that are coming. All four use Vellum's real dark colours and type. The markup tools shown (select, pen, line, box, cloud, text, measure) are placeholders for whatever the set turns out to be; the pill on the right shows the navigation mode picked for the document (CAD or Document).

A. Centred in the toolbar

Pages and zoom sit together in the middle, as in Acrobat and Edge. For: balanced, familiar, the two most-used controls are where the eye lands. Against: markup tools and search have to share the ends, so the toolbar fills up fast once tools arrive.

B. Pages left, zoom right

Each control near what it relates to: pages beside the sidebar's page list, zoom at the far end. Tools and search take the middle. For: scales well as tools grow, nothing crowds. Against: the two most-used controls are at opposite ends; zoom is a long mouse trip from the drawing's left half.

C. Floating bar at the bottom

Pages and zoom move into a small bar floating over the bottom of the drawing, as in Chrome's PDF viewer; the toolbar keeps the tools and search. For: navigation sits on the drawing where your eye is, and the toolbar is free for tools. Against: it covers a strip of the drawing (it could fade when the mouse is away), and it sits next to the mini map.

D. One top row, tools in a side rail

The menus, page navigator, zoom and search share one row, and the markup tools stand in a narrow rail down the left of the drawing, as in Bluebeam and most drawing apps. For: one row less at the top, so more drawing; tools get a home that can grow downwards with room for options; navigation sits centred in the row. Against: the menus and controls share a row, so a narrow window has to shed things (search collapses to an icon first); the rail takes 44 px of width.
